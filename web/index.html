Rendering

The geometry

Two circles in perpendicular planes make a torus on the unit 3-sphere. A rotation in four dimensions changes how that torus projects into our three-dimensional world.

X = (cos t cos u, cos t sin u,
sin t cos v, sin t sin v)

For a point p = (x, y, z), let V = (2x, 2y, 2z, |p|² − 1). With P = R diag(1, 1, 0, 0) Rᵀ, every point on the projected surface satisfies:

VᵀPV − cos²t (1 + |p|²)² = 0

Each camera ray produces a quartic polynomial. The renderer centers each ray line at its closest point to the origin and covers each half-ray with a near-distance and a reciprocal-distance coordinate. It isolates real roots between derivative roots in a Bernstein basis and polishes them against the homogeneous surface equation. A scaled residual and a positive-distance cutoff select finite forward hits. Stationary candidates handle tangencies; infinity is excluded. No finite bounding sphere, surface mesh or spatial ray marching is used.

Through the projection pole

The pole lies on the torus when P₄₄ = cos²t. At that instant, the projected surface is unbounded and the quartic drops degree. Use XW rotation to move through the transition. For an otherwise aligned solid, the pole crossing occurs at XW = 90° − t. “At pole” is shown within a small numerical neighborhood of the crossing.

Solid or shell

Solid torus fills f > 0. One surface bounds the glass, and its outward normal is −∇f. Entry and exit are different intersections with that same surface.

Torus shell puts glass between t₋ = t − Δt/2 and t₊ = t + Δt/2, so cos²t₊ < QᵀPQ < cos²t₋. Rays test both quartics at every bounce. The lower scalar boundary uses −∇f; the upper uses +∇f. Width is measured in the S³ parameter, not as a constant 3D wall thickness. The width control is limited near the degenerate endpoint circles. The shell can also extend through infinity when the pole lies inside its band.

Three tori, one Hopf family

Each torus has its own parameter, volume and material. The six rotations act on all three together. Materials follow the tori through the pole; they are not reassigned to keep a particular color on the inside. The visibility checkboxes let you inspect individual tori. When material regions overlap, the narrower interval in QᵀPQ takes priority. This keeps an inner solid’s material distinct from its surroundings. Equal intervals use the higher-numbered torus. Touching boundaries change media together.

Materials and environments

Chrome uses a tinted Schlick approximation for metal Fresnel reflectance. Its reflected rays intersect the other tori. Roughness broadens the distant environment reflection; it is not a complete microfacet transport model. Six procedural environments and locally loaded equirectangular panoramas share rotation and exposure controls. Panorama images stay in this browser tab.

Glass and light

Refraction follows Snell’s law at successive interfaces, with exact dielectric Fresnel reflectance, total internal reflection, and distance-dependent absorption. The first reflected branch also intersects the torus. Subsequent reflection branches sample the procedural environment.

Transport is bounded to 8, 16, or 24 interfaces. Remaining energy resolves to the environment; an unbounded material segment uses a finite absorption approximation. Single-precision arithmetic limits extremely distant and grazing intersections. Opacity blends local surface lighting with glass transmission.

Rotation order: XY, XZ, XW, YZ, YW, ZW (left multiplication). XY and ZW can leave an aligned torus unchanged because of its symmetry. Orbiting the camera changes only the 3D viewpoint.
